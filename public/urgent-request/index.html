First, the basics

How an scam works

An urgent-request scam is a message that uses pressure and a trusted name to rush you into sending money or data before you stop to check.

Explore all three tabs below to learn more about this social-engineering attack.

Attackers impersonate someone you trust, like a boss, a vendor, or a coworker, and they pile on pressure so you act before you think.

The goal is almost always money, card or login details, or sensitive data.

Five tells show up again and again. Hover or focus each one to see an example.

  • Extreme urgency or a tight deadline Example: "Wire this in the next 10 minutes before the window closes."
  • Pressure to keep it secret or skip normal steps Example: "Keep this between us until I tell you it is done."
  • A lookalike or external email domain Example: a note from payroll@acme-hr-portal.com
  • A request for money, card details, or login credentials Example: "Send the card number and CVV so I can pay the vendor."
  • A link or attachment to act on under pressure Example: "Open the attached invoice and enable editing to approve."

Not a red flag A sender using your first name. Personalization is normal in everyday email.

Do not act on the message itself.

Verify the request through a channel you already trust, like calling the person on a known number or checking in person. Then report it to your security team.

That is what the attacker wanted.

Sharing card details by message hands the attacker direct access to company funds. The pressure and secrecy were the tell. A real executive expects you to verify a money request.

Risky move.

Links sent under pressure can capture credentials, authorize payments, or install malware, even if they look legitimate. The pressure and time crunch are designed to stop you from checking. Verify the request through a known channel before clicking anything.

          Check the address

          The display name can say anything. What matters is the domain, the part right before .com.

          Real daniel.okafor@yourcompany.com
          Fake daniel.okafor@finance-corp-secure.com

          The word right before .com is who really owns the address. Yours is "yourcompany"; this sender's is "finance-corp-secure", an outside domain dressed up to look official.
